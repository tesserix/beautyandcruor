Beauty & Cruor

A mobile-first rebuild of Parimiti's portfolio. The current site spends its entire animation budget on desktop and renders one animated element at phone width — while still shipping the whole stack to phones. This direction inverts that: the photograph is the interface, and everything else gets out of its way.

Palette

Her work is already saturated — blood, gel lighting, body paint, teal wigs. So the interface goes warm-monochrome and stays there. The accent is drawn from the materials she actually works in: alginate. Cruor is Latin for clotted blood, so the red is the brand, not decoration — and it's rationed to one element per screen.

What drives the layout

The photograph is the interface

  • Full-bleed, no gutters. Container padding steals width that the work needs. Images run edge to edge; only text gets a margin.
  • Vertical-first crops. 71% of her library is portrait, dominant ratios 3:4 and 2:3. The grid is built for that shape rather than fighting it.
  • Swipe, never hover. Every hover reveal on the current site is dead weight on a phone. The hero and galleries are scroll-snap carousels that work by thumb.
  • The CTA never leaves. Enquiries are the point of the site, so the bar is pinned in the thumb arc on mobile and folds into the nav on desktop.

Motion

Spent on images, not decoration

  • No scroll reveals. The old build ran Waypoints and jQuery.appear to fade in blocks. Content is visible at rest; nothing waits on an observer.
  • Transitions between works — a crossfade and a slow scale on the image itself — carry the whole motion budget.
  • Native scroll-snap for carousels, so the gesture is the OS's, not a library's. Embla only if we need finer control.
  • prefers-reduced-motion honoured throughout; the page is fully usable with all motion off.

Needs a decision before build

  1. Alt text for 290 imagesEvery image in the library has an empty alt attribute. Lighthouse 100 on accessibility is unreachable without them, and they can't be migrated — someone has to write them. I can draft from the imagery for Parimiti to correct.
  2. Which work is publicA lot of the library is behind-the-scenes and phone snapshots. The prototype uses roughly 26 images I selected as portfolio-grade; Parimiti should confirm the real edit.
  3. 78 images below the retina floorThey're under 1200px wide, so they can't go full-bleed on a phone without softening. Either they sit in a smaller treatment or they're re-exported from originals if she still has them.
  4. The logo needs work to survive hereThe supplied mark is dark-grey script with a lime flourish, drawn for light backgrounds — on this ground it all but disappears, so the two variants in the control above are recoloured. It's also raster-only at 711×218, with no vector, and the botanical script reads closer to wellness than to prosthetics and trauma work. Options: use it recoloured, have it traced to SVG and redrawn, or set the name in type. Switch between all three above.
  5. The namePrototype leads with "Parimiti" and treats Beauty & Cruor as the studio mark. Confirm which is the masthead.
  6. Nine videos in the libraryIncluding a 24MB 3840×2160 .mov. If any belong on the site they need a separate encode pipeline — worth knowing now.
